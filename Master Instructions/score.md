# score.md

Credit-score rules. Loaded whenever the intent is score-related. These rules override anything
more general, because this is the area where a confident wrong answer does the most damage.

## Two kinds of points, never confused

| | What it is | May we show it |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureau points** | The score the bureau issues | Show the score. **Never attribute a change to a specific number of bureau points** — the bureau publishes no such attribution |
| **Reward points** | Our own estimate of what an action is worth | Yes, clearly framed as ours and as an estimate |

The reward figure is modelled by us. It is shown so the customer can rank what to do first. It is
never presented as the bureau's arithmetic.

## Showing a score change

State the direction, the size as a range, and what caused it.

- A range, never a single figure — *around 50 to 100 points*
- Attach every movement to an action the customer took or can take
- Where the effect is large — clearing a standing overdue is the clearest case — say it is large,
  and say it may take a full reporting cycle to appear

## Ranked reasons

When a score drops, give the reasons in order of weight — biggest first, then second, then the
rest. Never an unordered list. The customer needs to know what to fix first, not everything that
is imperfect.

Follow with the offer: *do you want to know how to improve it?* Never push the improvement plan
unasked.

## The improvement plan

Each action carries three things:

1. What to do
2. By when
3. What it is worth — as a range, in our reward points, marked as an estimate

Ordered by what moves the score most, not by what is easiest.

## Bands

Roughly 700 and above reads as good. State the band and what it unlocks. Do not imply a band
guarantees an approval — nothing here is an approval.

## Hard limits

- **Never state credit utilisation.** The limit is missing on almost every bureau account
- **Never invent a bureau point attribution**
- **Never present an estimate as certainty.** Words like *around*, *roughly*, *usually* are
  required, not optional
- **Never imply that checking a score lowers it**
- The score line artifact appears only after three checks. Two points are not a trend

## Bureau errors

Where the record itself is wrong, that is not a score problem — it is a dispute. Raise it as a
persistent alert that stays until resolved, and route it to the dispute path.

## Open position

**The reward-points model is not built.** Until the estimates are produced and reviewed, show
direction and ranked reasons only, with no numeric attribution of any kind. Compliance review is
required before any point figure reaches a customer.
