# language.md

Kannada, Hindi and English. Loaded wherever text is produced or read.

Communication in a language the borrower understands is a regulatory requirement here, not a
preference.

## Which language to reply in

Work down in order:

1. **The language of the message just sent** — including Latin-script Hindi or Kannada
2. **The language they last used in this thread**
3. **The language selected at onboarding**

The selection at onboarding sets the interface. **It does not override what they just typed.**

## Latin-script input

Typing Hindi or Kannada in Latin letters is normal, not an error. Treat *"mera score kyun gira"* as
Hindi.

**Reply in the script they used.** If they typed Hindi in Latin letters, reply in Latin letters.
Switching them into Devanagari mid-conversation is a worse experience than matching them.

## Switching mid-conversation

- Follow immediately. No confirmation, no comment on the switch
- Do not ask them to pick a language again
- Labels inside a card follow the reply language, not the onboarding setting

## Numbers, dates and money

| Item | Form |
|---|---|
| Amounts | Indian grouping. **₹1,04,200**, never ₹104,200 |
| Dates | *5 September*, never 05/09 |
| Periods | Name the month. Never "last month" without saying which |
| Percentages | One decimal place at most |
| Large amounts | Digits only inside a card. In text, digits |

## What stays in Latin script

Inside a Kannada or Hindi sentence, keep in Latin script:

- Lender, bank and product names
- Document names — PAN, Aadhaar, NOC, EMI, UPI, KFS
- Anything the customer will have to read off a physical document or type into a form

**Never mix two scripts in one word.** Mixing them across a sentence, where the list above applies,
is correct.

## Cards must survive translation

- **A card label means the same thing in all three languages, or the label changes.**
- Kannada and Hindi run roughly 40% longer than English. Design to the longest, not the shortest
- If a label overflows, **change the label — never shrink the type**
- Never abbreviate a label to make it fit
- Numbers inside a card never translate. Only their labels do

## Voice

- Transcribe in the language spoken
- **Show the transcript back before acting** where the message carries an amount, a date or a lender
  name
- Below the confidence floor, ask again. Do not act on a guess at a number
- Read-aloud, where offered, uses the reply language

## Never

- Never translate a lender's name
- Never translate a figure, a date or a reference number
- Never apologise for a language, or offer to switch to English "for accuracy"
- Never leave part of a reply in English because a term was hard to translate — find the plain word
- Never use a transliteration the customer would not recognise from their own documents

## Open position

**Vernacular copy does not exist.** Every fixed string, every card label and every intent phrasing
is currently English only. Kannada and Hindi are required before release, and no layout has yet
been reviewed in either.
